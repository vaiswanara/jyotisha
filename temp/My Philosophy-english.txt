Why I Teach Astrology — and How I Choose to Live
My approach to Astrology, Teaching, Dharma and Life
I have been asked many times:
“You teach Astrology. Why do you teach it freely instead of offering consultations and charging a fee?”
“What do you expect from your students?”
“If someone supports you financially, do you treat them differently?”
“Do you believe Astrology can change a person's life?”
“Why don't you make predictions?”
“What is your approach to remedies, prayers and donations?”
These are genuine questions. I would therefore like to explain my approach openly and respectfully.
This page is not intended to prove that my way is the only right way. It is simply an explanation of how I have chosen to learn, teach and live.
________________________________________
1. Why Did I Choose to Learn Astrology?
I did not learn Astrology with the intention of making money, gaining fame, or building a profession.
Astrology has been part of my family background. My father and grandfather were also astrologers. However, I did not learn Astrology simply because it was a family tradition.
Certain experiences in my life made me think deeply about the way Astrology is understood and used.
I came to realise that Astrology can influence a person's thinking very deeply. When it is explained responsibly, it can help a person understand a situation and face life with greater awareness.
But when it is used irresponsibly, it can create fear, dependency and blind belief.
That realisation became one of the reasons for my serious study of Astrology.
I wanted to understand the subject deeply so that I could share whatever I learned for the benefit and awareness of others.
________________________________________
2. Why Do I Teach Astrology?
My purpose is not to make people dependent on an astrologer.
My purpose is almost the opposite.
I want a student to learn Astrology well enough to observe, question, think and understand.
I therefore tell my students:
“Do not depend on my guidance more than you depend on the subject. Learn the subject, understand the principles and develop your own judgement.”
I believe knowledge becomes meaningful when it makes a person more independent.
________________________________________
3. I Teach Astrology; I Do Not Offer Personal Consultations
My primary role is that of a teacher.
I teach Astrology. I do not offer regular personal horoscope consultations.
If a student asks me a question about the subject, I am always willing to explain it. If the student asks the same question several times because it has not yet become clear, I do not consider that a problem.
Everyone has a different level of understanding.
My restriction is only regarding personal consultation, not regarding students' questions.
If someone approaches me with a genuine and serious personal difficulty, I may offer guidance within my limits.
Even then, I do not promise predictions.
My intention is to help the person understand the situation and think about possible ways of dealing with it.
________________________________________
4. Why Don't I Make Future Predictions?
I do not consider Astrology to be a tool for making a person passive.
A horoscope may help us understand:
•	the circumstances surrounding a person, 
•	favourable and unfavourable tendencies, 
•	psychological tendencies, 
•	the kind of environment a person may experience, 
•	and possible challenges or opportunities. 
But that does not mean that every decision a person makes is permanently fixed.
For example, a person's financial circumstances may determine which college he can initially join.
But his marks, rank, learning, discipline and final achievement are not automatically determined by his financial situation.
Circumstances influence us. They do not completely replace our effort and choices.
Therefore, I prefer the word Guidance rather than using Astrology merely as a tool for prediction.
The purpose of Astrology, in my approach, is not to tell a person what will happen so that he can simply wait for it. It is to help him understand his circumstances so that he can respond to them wisely.
________________________________________
5. Astrology Should Reduce Fear, Not Create Fear
When a person comes to an astrologer with a problem, he is often already anxious.
He may be waiting nervously to hear:
“What does my horoscope say?”
At such a moment, I believe an astrologer should not add more fear to an already troubled mind.
The responsibility of the astrologer is not merely to confirm the person's fear.
First, the problem must be understood.
Then we should ask:
“What can this person do?”
“What possibilities are available?”
“How can this person face the situation?”
If there is a practical way forward, that should be explained.
If the situation cannot be immediately changed, the person should be helped to develop the mental strength to face it.
This is why I consider psychological awareness to be an important part of Astrology education.
________________________________________
6. Astrology and Psychology
I believe that understanding life requires us to consider both time and human response.
Astrology helps us study the dimension of Kāla — Time.
Psychological understanding helps us understand how a human being thinks, feels and responds to circumstances.
A person may face a difficult situation.
Two people may experience a similar circumstance, but they may respond very differently.
One may become frightened.
Another may become stronger.
Therefore, understanding the circumstance alone is not enough.
We must also understand the mind.
That is why I include psychological awareness along with Astrology in my teaching.
________________________________________
7. When Should We Consult an Astrologer?
I tell my students something very simple:
“You do not need an astrologer for every problem in life.”
A person in perfect physical health does not need to visit a doctor every day.
Similarly, a person who is mentally strong and understands his situation does not need to approach an astrologer for every difficulty.
If you understand the cause of a problem and know what you need to do, then there may be no need to look at the horoscope.
Astrology becomes useful when:
“I do not understand why this situation is occurring, or I need another perspective to understand it.”
Even then, Astrology should support our thinking — not replace it.
________________________________________
8. What Do I Tell Students About Their Own Horoscopes?
When students begin learning Astrology, I generally advise them:
“Do not immediately use your own horoscope, or the horoscope of someone to whom you are emotionally attached, as your main learning example.”
Why?
Because if we see something we consider negative in our own horoscope, we may become emotionally disturbed.
Once fear enters the learning process, it becomes difficult to study objectively.
When we study a horoscope to which we have no emotional attachment, our mind can examine it more deeply — sometimes even critically.
Therefore, in my classes I use horoscopes as examples without disclosing whose horoscope it is.
The purpose is learning, not emotional involvement.
________________________________________
9. My Approach to Serious Personal Problems
If someone tells me:
“I am facing a genuine and serious problem and I do not understand what to do,”
I first try to understand the problem.
Sometimes a person may consider something extremely serious which can actually be handled with simple practical changes.
For example, a parent may say:
“My child's education is becoming a very serious problem.”
After discussing the situation, it may become clear that the child spends excessive time with television, smartphones or other distractions.
In such a situation, the first guidance should not be a horoscope or a ritual.
The first guidance may simply be:
Change the environment. Set appropriate boundaries. Encourage effort and discipline.
If human effort itself is missing, no ritual can become a substitute for that effort.
________________________________________
10. My Approach to Remedies
I respect traditional practices such as:
•	prayer, 
•	japa, 
•	vrata, 
•	puja, 
•	homa, 
•	shanti practices, 
•	and dana. 
But I do not consider them to be commercial quick fixes for every problem.
When a person is under severe mental pressure, prayer or other spiritual practices may help calm the mind and restore inner confidence.
Once the mind becomes calmer, the person may become capable of understanding the actual problem and taking appropriate action.
Therefore, I see such practices primarily as support for the mind and spirit, not as a replacement for human effort.
Prayer may strengthen the mind.
Effort must address the problem.
Divine Grace supports the journey.
I do not recommend remedies that require a person to spend money beyond their means.
A person does not have to spend a large amount of money to approach God.
Lighting a simple lamp with devotion and sincerely praying in one's own mother tongue may be more meaningful than spending beyond one's capacity on an elaborate ritual.
________________________________________
11. My Understanding of Prayer and Prāyaścitta
To me, prayer is not merely:
“I have a problem. Please remove it.”
There is another deeper dimension.
When a person recognises a mistake, sincerely decides not to repeat it, and turns towards a better way of living, that inner transformation is important.
Traditional spiritual practices can support such a transformation.
Therefore, I do not encourage people to depend on rituals while continuing the same actions that created the problem.
A remedy should lead to awareness and change — not become a substitute for change.
________________________________________
12. I Do Not Teach Death Prediction
There is one boundary I maintain clearly.
I do not teach or practise death prediction or death-related timing.
I do not want fear of death to become part of a student's learning process.
Astrology, in my approach, should help a person understand life and face it with awareness.
It should not become a source of unnecessary fear.
________________________________________
13. My Approach to Money and Amṛta Vṛtti
I am a householder.
I have a family and responsibilities.
Money is therefore a necessity, but I do not want it to become an obsession or the purpose of my teaching.
I have chosen what I understand as Amṛta Vṛtti as my way of life.
In simple terms, I do not ask people to give me money.
I do not fix a fee for my teaching.
If someone voluntarily supports my basic household needs, I receive it as:
“Sri Hari Sankalpa.”
I do not consider such support to be a payment for my teaching.
Nor do I expect it.
If someone wants to help but I do not need what they are offering, I would rather request them to help someone who genuinely needs it.
________________________________________
14. Financial Support Does Not Buy Special Treatment
This is important.
A student who supports me financially and a student who does not support me are equal in my teaching.
I do not maintain a list of:
•	who contributed, 
•	who did not contribute, 
•	how much someone contributed, 
•	or who should therefore receive special attention. 
My teaching is not dependent on financial support.
Support does not create entitlement to personal consultation.
And lack of support does not reduce a student's value.
________________________________________
15. Dāna and Sevā
I make a distinction between Dāna and Sevā.
Dāna is giving something that belongs to us, without expecting a personal return.
Sevā is offering our time, effort, ability or energy for the welfare of others.
Both have value.
I do not expect either one from my students.
I believe that helping society according to one's ability is itself a meaningful form of Dharma.
Paropakāra — helping others — also strengthens the person who serves.
________________________________________
16. Why I Do Not Treat Students Differently
There are hundreds of students in my learning groups.
I intentionally avoid creating a system where I know:
“This person supported me, and that person did not.”
Such a system can unconsciously create expectations and discrimination.
I want my relationship with students to remain centred on learning.
Therefore:
The classroom is for knowledge, not for financial relationships.
________________________________________
17. What Do I Expect From My Students?
I do not expect money.
I do not expect personal loyalty.
I do not expect them to depend on me.
What I hope for is much simpler:
Learn sincerely.
Ask questions freely.
Think independently.
Make your own effort.
Develop psychological strength.
Use Astrology responsibly.
Do not blindly follow any person or belief.
And, whenever possible:
Help others.
________________________________________
18. What Does “Astrological Awareness” Mean to Me?
For me, Astrological Awareness means coming out of:
•	blind belief, 
•	unnecessary fear, 
•	superstition, 
•	fatalistic thinking, 
•	and excessive dependence on predictions and remedies. 
A person should not think:
“My horoscope says this, therefore I cannot do anything.”
Instead, he should ask:
“What is my situation?
What is within my control?
What effort can I make?
How should I respond?
Where do I need Divine Grace?”
That shift in thinking is, for me, one of the most valuable purposes of learning Astrology.
________________________________________
19. Why I Want Students to Become Independent
A teacher should not create lifelong dependency.
During the learning process, I am happy to answer questions repeatedly and explain concepts as many times as necessary.
But the ultimate purpose is not:
“Ask the teacher about every problem.”
It is:
“Learn enough to understand the subject and think for yourself.”
My guidance should be a support during the learning journey — not a substitute for the student's own understanding.
________________________________________
20. Why I Do Not Seek Likes, Shares or Subscribers
There is another aspect of my approach that I would like my students to understand.
Today, earning money from astrology is not always done by directly charging a consultation fee. Social media recognition itself can become a form of reward. Likes, shares, subscribers, views, comments and channel growth can gradually become goals in themselves.
I have consciously chosen not to make them my goals.
I have uploaded 900+ astrology classes, many of them around one hour in duration. Some of my students have suggested that I enable YouTube monetisation for these videos.
I have consciously chosen not to do so.
There are two reasons for this.
First, monetisation may result in advertisements being displayed along with the educational content. I do not want my students to be unnecessarily distracted by advertisements, particularly advertisements that may have nothing to do with the subject they are learning.
More importantly, earning through these videos does not fit with the Amṛta Vṛtti that I have chosen for my life.
Therefore, I openly tell my students:
You do not have to like my videos.
You do not have to subscribe to my channel.
You do not have to share my videos or my channel.
You do not have to press the notification bell.
None of these things are expected from my students.
Then why do I upload the videos?
The purpose is very simple.
I want the students who are learning from me to have access to the lessons whenever they need them. Therefore, I upload the classes to YouTube and share the links with my students.
For me, YouTube is simply a means of delivering educational material, not a means of building popularity.
I do not normally promote my videos through WhatsApp Status or other social-media platforms. I share the video links with the students for whom the lessons are intended.
I do not want to spend my mind wondering:
•	Who liked my video? 
•	Who disliked it? 
•	Why did someone unsubscribe? 
•	Why did someone stop watching? 
•	How many views did the video receive? 
•	How many subscribers did the channel gain? 
If I start thinking about these things, my attention can gradually move away from teaching and learning towards recognition and numbers.
I would rather remain free from that pressure.
What do I expect from a student instead?
Nothing related to social-media promotion.
If you have learned something useful from these classes, understand it, practise it, experience it and, when appropriate, share that knowledge with someone who genuinely needs it.
That is more valuable to me than a thousand likes.
I do not want my students to promote me.
I would rather see them use what they have learned to help others.
That is much closer to the spirit in which I received this knowledge and the spirit in which I wish to pass it on to the next generation.
________________________________________
21. My Measure of Success
I do not measure the value of my teaching by the number of students, subscribers, views, likes or donations.
My satisfaction comes from knowing that I am continuing to learn, sharing what I have understood, and helping a student become sufficiently knowledgeable and confident to understand life for themselves.
If my teaching eventually makes a student less dependent on an astrologer, I consider that a success — not a failure.
And if that student uses the knowledge to bring clarity, courage and awareness to another person, the purpose of my teaching has been fulfilled.
That is the kind of continuity I wish to create — not a following, but a chain of knowledge.
________________________________________

22. My Simple Message to My Students
I would like my students to remember these few principles:
Keep your mind strong.
Understand your circumstances.
Do not fear your horoscope.
Do not surrender your life to predictions.
Give the first priority to your own effort.
Use prayer to strengthen the mind, not to avoid responsibility.
Seek Divine Grace without abandoning your duty.
Help others whenever you can.
And approach an astrologer only when you genuinely need another perspective.
________________________________________
Finally, Why Do I Do This?
I do not teach Astrology because I want people to remember my name.
I do not teach it because I want to build a commercial practice.
I do not teach it because I expect every student to support me.
I teach because the knowledge that came to us from our teachers and ancient traditions should not end with one generation.
My teachers gave me knowledge.
I did not pay them a commercial fee for that knowledge.
Therefore, I consider sharing what I have learned with sincere students to be a responsibility.
If, through my teaching, even one person becomes less fearful, more thoughtful, more self-reliant, more aware of his responsibilities, and more compassionate towards others, I consider that meaningful.
My aim is not to create a society that constantly needs astrologers.
My aim is to help create people who are strong enough to face life, wise enough to understand their circumstances, humble enough to seek Divine Grace, and compassionate enough to help others.
Learn deeply. Think independently. Live responsibly. Seek Divine Grace. Help others.
— Srikanth Dharmavaram
